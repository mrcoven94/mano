# Decisions

Settled on October 26, 2026. Change one only with David, and record the change here with the date.

## Product

1. **Promise:** apply once, for anything that makes your life better. Benefits, cash assistance, jobs, scholarships and training, plus any form a person hands to Mano.
2. **Who gets an agent:** every resident, every agency staff member, every nonprofit case manager. Same code, three role packs (skills, tools, permissions). An organization has an org agent whose policies and programs its staff agents inherit.
3. **Consent:** a resident shares a case only by explicit grant, to a specific organization, for a specific purpose. Either side can request documents. The resident can revoke future access at any time (revoking cannot recall what was already sent, and Mano says so).
4. **Authorized representatives:** consent in Mano is not legal representative status. Where a program requires a designation, the resident signs the program's own form; the resident attests, Mano assists.
5. **Office profiles, not office agents:** every government office has a labeled, independent profile from day one. A profile cannot converse, request records or send notices. When an office claims its profile, it becomes an org agent with staff seats.
6. **Honesty:** Mano never presents itself as a human or as the person. See AGENTS.md rule 3.
7. **Price:** free forever for applying, renewals, deadlines, reminders and filing; nothing that prevents someone losing help is paid. Plus is $8 a month or $60 a year for things that cost us money (calls on your behalf, mailed and faxed letters, a priority line). Organizations pay per staff seat.
8. **Partner extensions:** outside services plug in as extensions (tools, a skill, the exact data they ask for, who pays). The agent offers one only when it helps; the resident approves each one and can remove it. We never sell personal data.
9. **An app and a platform** (Oct 26): residents use a React Native app (Expo) that builds iOS, Android and the web from one codebase. Staff and case managers work on the web. Mano is also a platform: a public API, an SDK, the open protocol and the extension manifest let other organizations and developers build on it, and anyone can deploy their own copy.

## Hackathon scope (spine first)

The spine must run end to end from October 30 and every day after:

1. Maria photographs or films her mail; Mano splits it into letters, reads each one and builds her deadlines.
2. Mano fills out her renewal, she approves the exact version, and it is submitted to the sandbox agency.
3. The county caseworker's agent (an org that claimed its profile) asks for a missing pay stub; Maria answers with a photo.
4. The caseworker approves; the org agent enters the case into a mock legacy system by computer use; a notice goes out.
5. On camera: a document changes after approval and the stale approval is blocked; a submission times out and is reconciled without a duplicate; a planted scam link is caught.

Then, in order, while the spine stays green: phone and hold-for-me on our own sandbox line, iMessage and SMS, any-form filling on unseen forms, one program built from one real policy document and reviewed by a person, one partner extension on a public API, jobs and scholarships. The resident app ships on Android and the web for the hackathon; iOS goes to TestFlight only if Apple enrollment clears in time. Out of scope for the hackathon: payments, nationwide form learning.

## Proof

A held-out test, frozen before any tuning: renewal and document scenarios with unreadable pages, missing evidence, conflicting dates, planted instructions and scam links. Mano is compared with a plain reminder tool and a general agent with the same tools. Results report correct packets, missed deadlines, blocked unsafe actions, failures, cost and time. No claims about dollars saved or losses prevented. A real caseworker reviews sample packets on camera.

## Platform

- Engine on Cloudflare (per-person Durable Objects with their own SQLite, R2 for files, Browser Run, Sandbox, Email Routing, AI Gateway). Chosen over Vercel because calls on hold need hour-long connections, idle agents should cost nothing at 10 million people, and per-person databases isolate data by construction.
- Shared relational data in PlanetScale Postgres through Hyperdrive.
- Agent computers on Modal Sandboxes (sponsor credits, per-second billing, the same platform as the Glimmer guardian); Cloudflare Browser Run for simple browsing and login handoff. Muse Spark 1.3 drives the computer (Wonder's computer tool was not enabled on our key on Oct 26).
- Website and staff screens on Vercel (`apps/site`, Next.js). Resident app in `apps/app` (React Native with Expo; Android builds through EAS need no Apple account).
- Model calls go through Cloudflare AI Gateway once CF_ACCOUNT_ID and AI_GATEWAY_ID are set; until then the client calls api.meta.ai directly (Oct 26 smoke run confirmed the direct path with a synthetic prompt).

- **November 3, 2026, Guardian sees each action unredacted.** At David's request, after three review rounds on PR 108 kept finding the same thing: every value removed before Guardian is a place an attack can hide, and pattern matching cannot make removal both thorough and safe (a pattern that removes enough also erases an instruction written to look like a name, a street or a case number, and one that keeps every instruction misses values written a new way). Guardian receives each action as Mano would send it: the type, the recipient, the purpose, the payload with every field and its real value (a submit's name, birth date, address, phone, household size and income, and any text or claims), the document hash when a document is attached, every cited source with its quote, the consents on record, and whether the person or content asked. Nothing is removed or relabeled, and Engine no longer reads the profile at check time. Guardian is Meta's open-weight Muse Glimmer on our own Modal GPU. Our code stores nothing and logs no request or answer body (`packages/guardian/modal/app.py` has no prints of either, vLLM's offline engine there has no request logger, and errors name what failed, never the text). Modal kept the web function's inputs and outputs for up to 7 days until the entry below moved Guardian to a Modal Server, which Modal does not store. No rule forbids Guardian seeing the action, since it is the same content the person approves and the recipient receives. A personal form field that holds an email address or a link is still held before Guardian is asked, with a plain reason, because no real name, birth date, address or phone holds one; Guardian reading every field is the defense. Guardian's prompt treats the person's later yes as consent for the action's own recipient, needs no cited source for form answers, checks that the form fits its purpose and its numbers could be true, and blocks any other destination, an email address or a link that is not the recipient or consented, or an instruction in any field. This supersedes the redaction approach of PR 104 and of earlier rounds of PR 108.
- **November 3, 2026, Modal keeps no copy of what Guardian checks, once the old app is stopped.** At David's request (LAP-765), since Guardian now sees each action unredacted. Modal's security page (modal.com/docs/guide/security, Data retention) lists inputs and outputs of Functions, Web Functions included, as kept "Up to 7 days, then deleted", and request and response payloads of a Server as "Not stored" and "proxied directly to your container". It lists no workspace or app setting that shortens the 7 days. So Guardian is now one Modal Server (`@app.server`, named `check`) on the GPU in a new app, `mano-guardian-server`, running vLLM in-process behind the same single POST route, the same bearer token and Modal-Key/Modal-Secret proxy auth, and the same request and response bodies. There is no CPU web function in front and no `.remote` or `.spawn` call carrying an action, and the heartbeat Dict is gone. What the Server does not store: the action Engine sends, the verdict, and the model's review, by Modal or by our code (no prints, no log lines, uvicorn's access log off). What is stored: app and container logs of startup and errors, which name what failed and never the text (1 day on Starter, 30 on Team); Modal's app and container metadata; the model weights volume, shared by every Guardian app. The proxy terminates TLS and sees each request in flight. The old app, `mano-guardian`, is a web Function, and merging does not touch it, so Engine keeps working on its old URL. Until it is stopped, Modal keeps every input it receives for up to 7 days each, so the no-copy claim holds only after the steps below. Its old `mano-guardian-state` Dict held only a heartbeat time and is deleted by Modal 7 days after its last write. The order David follows: (1) merge, which deploys `mano-guardian-server` and prints its URL, `<workspace>--mano-guardian-server-check.us-east.modal.direct` (a Server answers on `modal.direct`; the same label on `modal.run` returns 404); (2) copy that URL into the `MANO_GUARDIAN_URL` GitHub secret and its Infisical copy, and into the agent's and the quality nightly's copies; (3) redeploy Engine; (4) check that Engine's `/health` shows `checker: guardian` and run one live check; (5) run the `guardian-modal` workflow from `main` with target `stop-old`, which stops `mano-guardian`. Inputs it already holds are deleted 7 days after each was received. Behavior changes with the Server: with no container up, Modal's proxy answers 503 at once instead of queueing, and Engine reads that as a cold start ("try again in a few minutes", and warm-up state `starting`). On staging the proxy also answered 503 once for about a second right after the container became ready, so a check that gets 503 retries once after a second when a full check (7 seconds) still fits its 10 second budget; a warm-up never retries. Staging measured cold starts of 186 and 209 seconds and warm checks of 4.4 to 6.3 seconds. The 10-minute warm hold is now the Server's `scaledown_window` of 600 seconds after the last request, and every check already sends a warm-up. The `guardian-modal` workflow's `staging` target deploys main's code to `mano-guardian-staging`, to check a merged change before Engine's URL moves to it. Every target is written to run only from `main`, since each holds production Modal credentials and branch code run with them could deploy over production. Each job's condition checks the ref and its first step checks it again in the shell, because GitHub's `==` ignores case and would admit a branch named `MAIN`. Jobs carry no services, container, env or other keys the runner would set up with a secret before that step. `guardian-check` compares the workflow's keys, permissions and triggers, and each job's keys, condition, runner, environment and first step, with an exact allowlist, which catches accidental edits on `main` and in reviewed pull requests. None of this stops a hostile branch, whose run uses its own copy of both workflows. Only the `guardian-production` environment's deployment branch policy, limited to `main`, closes that. - **November 2, 2026, Mano asks instead of guessing, and the declaration is the person's.** Propel had general AI agents fill out the online SNAP application in two states (April 2026, an internal test with no real applicants and nothing submitted, https://www.propel.app/insights/can-ai-agents-help-people-apply-for-snap-part-1/): they guessed missing fields, cut a mistyped phone number to 10 digits instead of asking, and ticked the legal attestation themselves. Mano's renewal answers each one. Every value comes from the person's own answers or their profile (where a letter's facts land) and passes the same check either way; one that is missing or does not fit its field becomes one plain question, one at a time, in the form's order of importance: case number, name, date of birth, household size, income, address, phone, then the proof document. A value on file that does not fit is named in the question, never shortened or changed. Text goes to the portal exactly as given, less spaces around it; only dates and amounts are written the way the field stores them. A run of spaces inside a text value becomes one space when the value is taken, because a double space carries no meaning on a form and the text line shows it as one, so the person sees and approves the single-spaced value that is filed. A new call to fill the renewal from a later turn closes the version already shown before it asks anything, so a YES to that version never files a value the person has since corrected. A call that ends without holding a new version (it asks a question instead) also closes the drafts its own turn made, so the reply never carries a question for a version the person corrected in the same message. While a renewal the person already approved has no outcome yet (no receipt, and not failed), the form tools prepare nothing new, and Mano tells her plainly that it is still being checked with the office, that it will not be sent twice, and that she can change it once it settles; a later YES may still send the version she approved, once, under its key. Engine marks a filing failed only when the portal refused it or, through a compare-and-set on the attempt's version read before recovery's lookup and checks, when it was confirmed absent, so a receipt always wins and a request that moved the attempt keeps its outcome; a failed filing counts as settled and the pointer to its question is cleared, so a correction then goes through without another YES. Form calls within one turn run one at a time, and however a correcting call ends, a thrown error included, the drafts it was correcting are closed; one Engine does not confirm closed, after one retry, is held back, and one check (presentable) decides every question shown or counted as shown, so a held-back draft is never shown or made answerable. When a correction in a later turn cannot close the version she was shown, Mano tells her plainly that it still waits, that a YES would send it as it was, and to send the correction again; her next form call tries to close it again. A proposal a newer one replaced is never executed again: a replayed YES, a YES that waited on the safety check, and recovery all refuse it after reconciling, so a filing that went through still answers its receipt. A YES aimed at a question that has since been replaced shows nothing new: it says the question changed and nothing was sent, since only a question that went out with the reply of the turn that prepared it, and that Channels reported, can be answered. The in-memory hold-back stays as an extra guard, but safety does not depend on it. A form call that finds an approved filing whose post failed retires it in Engine before anything else, so a replayed YES cannot file it even when the correction only asks a question; Engine retires only an attempt still failed at the version it read, with no receipt (one a concurrent YES left in flight or unknown stays approved and the correction waits), a retired approval is still reconciled but never run again, and if the retire cannot be confirmed the tool prepares nothing and says so. A turn's reply shows a question only for a draft of that turn that its last form call for the form left waiting, read from the run's own tool results, so a replay that calls no tool shows nothing, a correction that failed shows nothing in any instance, and a result carried over from another turn is ignored. A receipt under the key wins over every refusal: every gateway answer about the person's own approval (superseded, retired, declined, already used) and every agent status read (the model's request status, the answers to YES and NO) reports it as sent, with its confirmation, whenever a receipt exists. A share's consent effect (a grant or revocation) is applied by the gateway in the same transaction that records the share's receipt and marks its attempt done, so no crash can leave a receipt whose effect never ran, and nothing applies it later on an answer. A grant's effect never applies once a revocation for that case is recorded, so a grant that reconciles after its revocation restores nothing. It is marked by action key in that transaction, so a later receipt for the same key (a late reconcile) applies nothing and a revoked consent stays revoked. Every start marks the share receipts already on file, since the earlier code applied those effects when they were recorded. Every form call first records, durably in Engine, that its turn is correcting the form: Engine then refuses drafts for that form from turns received before it, like a replaced draft, and closes those still waiting, so an older turn that resumes after a correction can never become the version a YES files. If the fence cannot be recorded, that exit cleans up like every other: the turn's earlier drafts for the form are closed or held back. A consent write that fails inside the settle transaction rolls the receipt, the done attempt and the marker back with it, so the next lookup applies it once; and a replayed filing answers its receipt before any fence refusal. Once every earlier filing for the form is settled (filed, retired or failed), a pointer to its question is cleared, so a correction goes ahead as a new filing. The renewal ends with the office's declaration under penalty of perjury. The draft freezes it word for word, the approval question shows it and says only the person can make it, and their YES makes it. Engine attaches who answered from the approval itself, never from caller input, and the sandbox executor files only with that answer from the applicant, sending the declaration as `{ statement, madeBy: "applicant" }`; the portal refuses any filing without it, and its browser pages have nothing to tick. Because a policy that allows submits without a YES has no answer to carry, such a policy can no longer file a renewal to the sandbox portal. The renewal also asks for the case number, read from the person's one food benefits case on file.
- **November 3, 2026, a texted photo is stored in a form the portal takes (LAP-743).** An iPhone camera saves photos as HEIC by default and iMessage can carry that file as is, and the sandbox portal takes only PDF, PNG and JPEG, so a HEIC letter could be read but never filed. Channels now reads each texted file's media type from its bytes, never from the provider's label, and stores a HEIC, HEIF or WebP photo as a JPEG copy (longest side at most 4096 pixels, quality 85) made by Cloudflare Images through the Worker's `IMAGES` binding. Images is a plain image service, not a model. A pure WASM decoder was measured and rejected: libheif's bundle is 2.0 MB (718 KB gzipped) before any JPEG encoder, which fits the Workers size limit, but decoding one synthetic 4032 by 3024 HEIC in Node on a laptop took about 220 ms of CPU and grew the process from 32 MB to about 182 MB (the decoder's heap plus a 48 MB RGBA copy), past the 128 MB a Worker isolate may use, and the decoder is LGPL. The real binding converted the same file in about 0.6 seconds of wall time, outside the Worker's CPU and memory, and gave identical bytes on a second run. Which form each file is stored in (the copy or the original) is saved in the conversation before the upload, and a replayed turn reuses it: a photo stored once is never converted again, one file never becomes two documents, and a copy that cannot be made again on replay stores nothing rather than the original. The ask for a screenshot is its own text after the reply, so the reply's length cap never drops it, and it is never added to a crisis reply. Logs name only a fixed set of bare media types, since a provider's label can carry the file's name. When no copy can be made (no binding, an Images error, a copy over 10 MB), Channels stores the photo as sent so Mail can still read it, and the same reply asks for a screenshot instead of leaving the problem for filing time. The conversion counts against Images' unique transformations.

The text line's plain responder asks it once when the Meta Model API answers 402, a 5xx or times out, whenever the `OPENROUTER_API_KEY` secret is set on Channels (`packages/channels/src/open-weight.ts`). - **November 3, 2026, a reminder the day before an interview or hearing (LAP-812).** The spine promises a reminder the day before Maria's interview, and the nudges were 14, 7 and 2 days before and on the day. A deadline the person shows up for (kind interview or hearing) is now nudged 14 and 7 days before, the day before and on the day, at the nudge time in the person's zone, through the same reminder path. The day before replaces 2 days out rather than joining it, so the person is not texted three days running. Paperwork kinds (renewal, recertification, redetermination, appeal, payment, documents) and deadlines with no kind keep 14, 7, 2 and the day of: the 2-day nudge leaves time to gather and send papers, which a day-before text would not add to. A nudge for a date the person shows up for only says when ("Your Medi-Cal interview is tomorrow."), since "is due" and "Want me to start it?" fit paperwork, not an appointment. Each send of the same deadline, a replay included, plans from the nudges stored for it, never from the kind it had before: the unsent ones stay, a day the kind no longer has is dropped, and a missing day is added while its nudge time is still ahead. Each nudge row records the due date it counts down to. A day handed off for a date, or cancelled by the person, is never planned again for that date at any revision, and a waiting nudge for a settled day is dropped rather than kept, so flipping the kind or moving the date away and back cannot send the same nudge twice. Nudges a change replaces are marked withdrawn, not cancelled, so a cancelled nudge of an open deadline is always one the person stopped. An interview or hearing tracked before this change moves to the new days and words the next time its key is sent. Rows stored before the due date column keep it empty rather than guessing it from their window, since the person's zone may have changed since; such a row that was handed off or cancelled settles its day count for that deadline whatever the date. That is conservative: it may skip a nudge, never repeat one.
- **November 3, 2026, sharing a case is per organization.** PR 120 recorded a revocation by case alone, so stopping with one organization blocked another's grant for that case. Decision 3 makes each grant specific to one organization and purpose, so the records are too (`revoked_shares`, by organization and case). Revoking stays final for that organization and case, so a grant that reconciles late can never restore access; sharing again is a new yes that opens a new case, which shows nothing of the old one. A grant carries the exact values the person read in the approval question, and that is what the organization holds; a document's bytes stay with the person's agent and both sides check the grant on every read. A document grant covers the hash the yes approved, in that conversation. Deleting the conversation ends every grant and closes open requests, but does not end the share: the organization keeps the case ID, the purpose, the values the person approved and its request and event history, plus a private record of which documents it was sent that staff no longer see, until the person stops sharing, and Mano cannot recall anything its staff saved. Channels forgets how to reach the person in the delete, so Mano says this in its own text after their next message, sent again until Channels confirms one. Someone who never texts again does not hear it. A grant that reconciles after a delete counts too, and a document whose delivery reconciles after a delete opens nothing. Staff ask from a closed list of document kinds and Mano words the text, so staff typing never reaches a person's phone. A photo sent while a request waits is treated as the answer and asked about with a YES, instead of being read as a letter. Staff sign in on the sandbox portal with its existing token, and Engine turns that into a short staff session for the sandbox organization and no other. Only that test office can be chosen for now; a real agency or partner comes with its own staff sign-in and its own written agreement (rule 9).
- **November 3, 2026, a due date's year is checked against today, not read from the person's words (LAP-874).** A production trace saved "November 24" as 2024-11-24, which plans no nudges; reading years out of the person's words kept finding new gaps in review, so the tools use only the ISO date the model passes (anything else asks), today in the zone of the person's state, and `yearConfirmed`.
A date ahead within 183 days is saved. A past date moves to its next occurrence if that is within 183 days, with a note to state the year, else asks. A date further ahead asks with both dates when a nearer occurrence is still to come, else is saved with the note. `yearConfirmed`, set only after the person answers, saves the date as given. A date past Engine's two-year limit asks, confirmed or not. A wrong reminder the person can correct is cheap; a lost deadline is not.
Every saved date is said with its year, and the model is told today's date each turn. Mail reads a letter's date from its own text, so an old letter's past date stays on record as "was due" and the reply says no reminder will go out.
- **November 3, 2026, Mano never sets an iMessage chat background (LAP-850).** David's decision: the background Mano could put on a person's thread looked bad and is not set again. The upload path, its settings (`MANO_CHAT_BACKGROUND_ENABLED`, `MANO_CHAT_BACKGROUND_URL`) and the Channel method that set one are deleted, so no setting can turn it back on; it had been off by default since October 29, but threads, the maintainer's included, got it before then. Blooio's v4 API can remove one (`DELETE /chats/{chatId}/background`), so a chat whose stored record says an earlier version confirmed it set a background has it removed once, after the next reply reaches the person, outside the turn, never on a crisis turn, after STOP or on a closed chat. Removing is idempotent, so a failed try is repeated on a later reply, at most 3 times per chat, the cap the uploads had. The `chat_backgrounds` table stays in each conversation's SQLite and records the removal (`clearing`, then `cleared`); nothing deletes its rows, and it is kept across a delete so the removal runs at most once. A record left uncertain (an upload whose outcome was unknown) is left alone: that upload may have failed and the person may have set their own background since, and removing theirs is worse than leaving one of ours in place.
- **November 3, 2026, Mano's own email inbox (LAP-858).** Offices write to a person's private address, `letters+<token>@<MANO_EMAIL_DOMAIN>`, handled by an `email` handler in `apps/channels`, since a letter has to end in a turn on the person's own conversation and Channels already owns that. The token is 130 random bits, minted by the conversation on its first turn with the inbox on and never built from a name or number; a global table maps it to the conversation, and the conversation keeps its own copy, so a delete ends the address at once. Cloudflare allows a catch-all only on an apex domain and otherwise one literal rule per address, so one literal rule for `letters@` with subaddressing on carries every token; a catch-all on the apex was the alternative, and it would have given the whole of withmano.com to this Worker. An email is evidence and never permission (rule 4): its words are stored as a `text/plain` document, Mail reads that as one page whose transcription is the text itself, with the same field checks, instruction flags and scam screen as a photo, and the turn carries the document and no words. The text to the person is fixed wording with nothing from the email in it. Links, including those hidden behind other words or written in entities in HTML, are looked up on arrival, before any cutting (rule 6); one that is blocked or cannot be looked up, or any scheme that is not a web link, gets a warning and is never repeated. HTML is read with htmlparser2, a maintained parser that runs on Workers, after each round of review found a new way past a hand-written scanner. When an email has both parts, the plain part is read along with any HTML sentence it does not contain, because a plain part is often a stale copy and a correction may be only in the HTML. At most 2 million characters and 20,000 tags are read, counted before parsing; the parser's work on close tags grows with open tags, and 2 MB of hostile tags took 39 seconds uncapped. Anything past that counts as a cut letter with failed links. A sender that fails DMARC, or a message without Cloudflare's own Authentication-Results first, is refused; a domain that publishes no DMARC is accepted, since many county offices publish none, and the sender's name is never shown to the person as proof of who wrote. Automatic mail and bounces are dropped silently so two systems never answer each other; every other refusal bounces, so a sender is not left thinking it arrived. Limits are 15 MB a message (10 MB is the most Engine stores in one file, about 13.7 MB as base64), 5 files, 10 emails a day per person, and the existing daily byte caps. An email is stored whole or bounced: one that does not fit the day's bytes bounces entirely. Words past 20,000 characters are cut, since a model reads them. When any part goes unread (cut words, a file left out, a PDF, a part that fails to decode or read), every file read from that email is marked in its media type, holds no date, and the person is told, because the unread part could cancel a date in what was read. One table counts email: a row per message and generation is a day's slot and its bytes, so a redelivery adds nothing, a crash leaves a row its redelivery reuses, and nothing from before a delete counts. An arrival text that does not go is retried one per alarm, never ahead of the person's own input, doubling from a minute to an hour for about a day, then given up with a log line; a STOP, close or delete ends it. Each try is sent once and never replayed by the turn machinery, under a key of its own, so a try that looked lost but landed can mean a second notice. Nothing an office wrote disappears without marking the email: the subject is always read, even alone, and the one part left out without a mark is an image, by its own bytes, embedded beside the words and under 30 KB, taken for a logo. That is a judgment on size alone, and a tiny image holding text would go unread. Any other part left out marks the email, including a signature file such as S/MIME, so a signed email holds no date. A batch with more files than one turn takes runs as more turns. PDFs are stored but not read, because Mail reads images and text and has no PDF renderer yet; the text says so and asks for a photo. Anyone who has the address can write to it, so what comes by email is read and never sent: an email gets a turn of its own, never answers a caseworker's request, is never automatic proof on a filing, and the gateway blocks any action naming one, so a stranger's mail cannot start a question that sends something. If a request is open, the reply says what it is and that the person answers it with their own photo. Their YES on a question built on someone else's document would be weak consent, and the wording could only have said they sent it. Outbound email is out of scope. `MANO_EMAIL_DOMAIN` stays unset, so Mano offers no address and refuses all mail, until routing is on.
- **November 3, 2026, a false scam alarm does not take away a real deadline (LAP-878).** The scam screen in `packages/mail/src/safety.ts` is plain rules on a letter's words, so it cannot tell a real payment vendor or a county portal on a plain `.org` from a scammer's site. There are two tiers. A clear scam is an ask no real office makes or a sender that is not one: payment by gift card, wire, crypto or a payment app, a fee to keep or renew SNAP or Medicaid, a Social Security number, PIN or password by text or email, an agency name no government has, a link that hides where it goes (a name before the @ sign, a bare address, a lookalike letter, a government name on a host that is not one), or urgency words with a demand and an unofficial link. Mano sends the plain warning first. If the letter states exactly one date, Mano adds that the letter claims it, that Mano has not confirmed it, and to check with the office at a phone number or office the person already knows. With more than one date, Mano names none and says to check. It holds nothing and sets no reminder from such a letter. So a false alarm in this tier is a recoverable annoyance and a real scam's date is harmless. A suspect link is an ask real offices also make (pay, verify, log in, give a Social Security number) sent to a link that is not on a government domain or Mano's short list of state sites. Mano warns about the link, names the date only as what the letter claims, tells the person to check it with their office at a phone number or office they already know, and holds no deadline until the person says the office confirmed it. So in both tiers a real date stays in front of the person, and a scam's date is never stated as fact. The cost is that a scam in the second tier gets no "scam" label, and a real deadline in either tier gets no reminders unless the person says the office confirmed it. Premiums, copays, share of cost and CHIP enrollment fees are not flagged on their own, since those charges are real.
- **November 3, 2026, Mano checks every day for new help, only if the person asks (LAP-859).** A person who asks gets a daily check on their own agent's Durable Object alarm: their profile against `packages/programs`, with `screenIncome` and no model call. A new match is one text, once per program and at most one a day, through the same delivery path as a deadline's nudge, between 9:00 and 20:00 in the zone of their state. It only ever says "may" and what to check, because the catalog never proves anyone qualifies: the office decides. It needs the state, a household size, and every income entry with an amount and a frequency; a program already on their profile is not news. The household size is `householdSizeOf` in `packages/programs`, the same rule forms use: whom the person named, or the number they gave, so a letter cannot widen the screen (rule 4). A text is screened again right before it goes, in case it waited overnight, and a profile write that lands during that read holds it for the next run. Channels cannot read the flag or the person's hours, so this text alone is marked no replay: one POST to the provider, and a send not accepted is dropped, never resent after they said stop. Every other reminder keeps Channels' retries. `MANO_DAILY_CHECK_SENDS` in Engine's `wrangler.toml` ships off, because an older Channels would store a replayable row: it is turned on in its own one-line pull request once both Workers have deployed this code, and turned off the same way. While off, the check runs, sends nothing and marks nothing raised. The catalog covers eight states, so anyone elsewhere gets no text, and the tool tells the agent to say so plainly. The flag is `person.alwaysOn` on the profile, set only by the person through the agent. Nothing in the code knows who pays for Plus, so the flag is the only gate today; the pricing page lists this under Plus, and gating it means adding that one check when Plus exists. - **November 3, 2026, faxes and mailed letters through the gateway (LAP-860).** Lob sends letters and Telnyx sends faxes; neither is a model (rule 13), and which account each deployment uses is its own configuration. Both sit behind the existing executor interface in `apps/engine/src/gateway` and stay off until their settings are present. Rule 2 is in code: a fax goes only to one recipient, our own number from config, and a letter only to one recipient, Lob's test mode, where nothing is printed or mailed. Mail reads `LOB_TEST_API_KEY` and refuses any key without the `test_` prefix, so real mail takes a code change and a new decision, and its receipts are labeled simulated. Engine works out the pages and the price from the stored document and freezes them in the request, over anything a caller wrote, so the question shows them before the yes and the yes binds them. The prices are estimates from the providers' published rates, overridable by setting, and nothing bills a person yet, so the question says the person is not charged. Each PDF that goes out starts with a cover page carrying "Prepared with Mano, sent with <name>'s approval." on its own page, so it never covers the person's document. A PDF goes out only if it is plain: anything a viewer could show differently from what is sent (any form field, optional layers, any annotation or link, a dynamic form, a page transparency group, a rotation, a crop, a page away from the origin) is refused before the question, and the person is asked for a photo instead. Completed PDF forms come back when there is a renderer to compare the result against. A fax or letter carries nothing beside its document, so a payload field that would show in the question and not go out is refused. A photo is turned to match its EXIF orientation, and a mirrored one is refused. Lob dedupes on an idempotency key and is looked up by metadata. Telnyx's fax endpoint takes no idempotency key, so the key travels in `client_state`, the lookup scans our own number's list, and each send claims a row in the person's SQLite before it goes, in one step with checking its attempt has not moved on, so a second run cannot send beside it. A fax the lookup does not list may still land, so no wait turns it into absent: it stays unknown until a lookup finds it, Mano tells the person it could not confirm the fax went, and only a fresh approval can send it again. The recovery sweep looks less often as time passes and stops at a day; a person's own YES still looks. Each attempt records the provider and account it began against, as a truncated hash of the key, and with either gone or changed it stays unknown in any mode instead of reaching another executor. The provider's copy of a faxed document is an upload that expires after an hour. Webhooks are verified by each provider's signature over the raw body with a five-minute window, and stay closed without a secret; they record status and do not yet tell the person. Lob redacts metadata and description in its webhooks by default (its "Using webhooks" page), so a letter is placed by its own ID, through a route Engine writes to storage when Lob answers. An event Engine cannot place gets a non-2xx answer, and Lob retries those with backoff. There is no model tool for these yet, because with rule 2 in force a resident has nowhere real to send to.

- **November 3, 2026, Mano waits on hold and connects the person (LAP-861).** Mano places a call the person approved, waits on hold, and rings the person's phone and bridges them when a human answers. The office's name and the reason are the approval's recipient and purpose, and the number and any menu keys are in its payload, so the person's YES is bound to all four. Mano plays nothing on the line: it has no speech output and rule 13 allows no third-party speech, so `CallProvider` has no method that plays sound and the office hears silence until the person is bridged. A person who answers may hang up on that silence, and this is unmeasured. Three limits are enforced in code, in `src/calls/hold-calls.ts`, both before the approval is drafted and right before dialing: the number must be on `MANO_CALL_ALLOWED_NUMBERS`, which holds only our own numbers (rule 2); only `MANO_CALL_PERSON_ID` may use this; and the only phone rung is `MANO_CALL_PERSON_NUMBER`, which no request can change. Each call's state lives on the person's Durable Object, as a pure state machine (`src/calls/hold-call.ts`) with durable timers, so a call survives eviction. Webhooks are deduped by event ID, and a late or repeated input changes nothing, so nothing rings or bridges twice. Hold audio is transcribed in memory in short windows, and each transcript is reduced to one boolean by plain code that checks for recording phrases first and greeting phrases second. Words on the line can only ring the person (rule 4). No audio or transcript is stored or logged. Outcomes the person must hear about (no one after the hold limit, default 60 minutes; they did not answer within the ring limit, default 45 seconds; the office hung up; three failed listening windows in a row, so a transcription outage does not sit on hold for an hour) hang up and text them on the reminder path, which already retries and dedupes. State commits before its effects run, so a crash between the two strands the call until its hold limit, which is armed before the first dial. After a dial with an unknown outcome the gateway stays blocked until a webhook proves the call happened, and it never redials. Effects recheck the call's state right before they act, because an event can land between a step and its effects, so a call that ended meanwhile never dials or bridges. Timers carry the attempt they were armed for, so a timer from a dial the provider refused cannot end the retry. Deleting the conversation ends a live call and blanks who was called, but keeps the row's opaque IDs, so a leg that a pending dial or a late webhook names is still hung up. A reconciled call's receipt carries the provider's simulated label, so a stand-in call never reads as real. Everything that awaits before the first dial stores nothing, and the generation check, the row and the start of the dial happen with no await between them, so a delete during setup never rings an office and a failed setup leaves nothing behind. A dial whose outcome is unknown blocks a replacement after its hold limit, since it may still be up, until a webhook names its leg or the provider's cap has passed; after the cap it reads as absent and the attempt settles as failed with no redial, because the gateway's recovery would otherwise redial on its own hours after the person was told the call failed; only a new approval dials again. A later hung_up counts as proof with no second hang up. Both legs follow one set of rules: a leg is none, open or down, a dial marks it open before the provider is called, a webhook proving a leg beats any error from its dial, and a call blocks a replacement until it has ended and no leg is open, or until the later of its legs' caps has passed. A hang up that fails is not retried, so that leg waits for a webhook or its cap. A delete ends every call in one step before any hang up runs, and a failed effect ends the call only if the call is still where that effect belongs, checked again after each await, so a late failure never tears down a connected call. Status: this runs only against a fake provider and a fake transcriber in tests. No phone provider API key or number exists in our vault, and Muse Voice Transcribe answers HTTP 402, so `createCallServices` returns null outside tests, `/webhooks/calls` answers 404, `/health` reports `calls: "off"`, and a call action behaves as it did before this change. Not built, because each needs those: the provider's implementation, its audio stream into `holdCallAudio`, the transcriber, and the agent's tool for proposing a call.

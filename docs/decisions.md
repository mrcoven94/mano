# Decisions

Settled on October 26, 2026. Change one only with David, and record the change here with the date.

## Product

1. **Promise:** apply once, for anything that makes your life better. Benefits, cash assistance, jobs, scholarships and training, plus any form a person hands to Mano.
2. **Who gets an agent:** every resident, every agency staff member, every nonprofit case manager. Same code, three role packs (skills, tools, permissions). An organization has an org agent whose policies and programs its staff agents inherit.
3. **Consent:** a resident shares a case only by explicit grant, to a specific organization, for a specific purpose. Either side can request documents. The resident can revoke future access at any time (revoking cannot recall what was already sent, and Mano says so).
4. **Authorized representatives:** consent in Mano is not legal representative status. Where a program requires a designation, the resident signs the program's own form; the resident attests, Mano assists.
5. **Office profiles, not office agents:** every government office has a labeled, independent profile from day one. A profile cannot converse, request records or send notices. When an office claims its profile, it becomes an org agent with staff seats.
6. **Honesty:** Mano never presents itself as a human or as the person. See AGENTS.md rule 3.
7. **Price:** free forever for applying, renewals, deadlines, reminders and filing; nothing that prevents someone losing help is paid. Plus is $8 a month or $60 a year for things that cost us money (calls on your behalf, mailed and faxed letters, a priority line). Organizations pay per staff seat.
8. **Partner extensions:** outside services plug in as extensions (tools, a skill, the exact data they ask for, who pays). The agent offers one only when it helps; the resident approves each one and can remove it. We never sell personal data.
9. **An app and a platform** (Oct 26): residents use a React Native app (Expo) that builds iOS, Android and the web from one codebase. Staff and case managers work on the web. Mano is also a platform: a public API, an SDK, the open protocol and the extension manifest let other organizations and developers build on it, and anyone can deploy their own copy.

## Hackathon scope (spine first)

The spine must run end to end from October 30 and every day after:

1. Maria photographs or films her mail; Mano splits it into letters, reads each one and builds her deadlines.
2. Mano fills out her renewal, she approves the exact version, and it is submitted to the sandbox agency.
3. The county caseworker's agent (an org that claimed its profile) asks for a missing pay stub; Maria answers with a photo.
4. The caseworker approves; the org agent enters the case into a mock legacy system by computer use; a notice goes out.
5. On camera: a document changes after approval and the stale approval is blocked; a submission times out and is reconciled without a duplicate; a planted scam link is caught.

Then, in order, while the spine stays green: phone and hold-for-me on our own sandbox line, iMessage and SMS, any-form filling on unseen forms, one program built from one real policy document and reviewed by a person, one partner extension on a public API, jobs and scholarships. The resident app ships on Android and the web for the hackathon; iOS goes to TestFlight only if Apple enrollment clears in time. Out of scope for the hackathon: payments, nationwide form learning.

## Proof

A held-out test, frozen before any tuning: renewal and document scenarios with unreadable pages, missing evidence, conflicting dates, planted instructions and scam links. Mano is compared with a plain reminder tool and a general agent with the same tools. Results report correct packets, missed deadlines, blocked unsafe actions, failures, cost and time. No claims about dollars saved or losses prevented. A real caseworker reviews sample packets on camera.

## Platform

- Engine on Cloudflare (per-person Durable Objects with their own SQLite, R2 for files, Browser Run, Sandbox, Email Routing, AI Gateway). Chosen over Vercel because calls on hold need hour-long connections, idle agents should cost nothing at 10 million people, and per-person databases isolate data by construction.
- Shared relational data in PlanetScale Postgres through Hyperdrive.
- Agent computers on Modal Sandboxes (sponsor credits, per-second billing, the same platform as the Glimmer guardian); Cloudflare Browser Run for simple browsing and login handoff. Muse Spark 1.3 drives the computer (Wonder's computer tool was not enabled on our key on Oct 26).
- Website and staff screens on Vercel (`apps/site`, Next.js). Resident app in `apps/app` (React Native with Expo; Android builds through EAS need no Apple account).
- Model calls go through Cloudflare AI Gateway once CF_ACCOUNT_ID and AI_GATEWAY_ID are set; until then the client calls api.meta.ai directly (Oct 26 smoke run confirmed the direct path with a synthetic prompt).

- **October 30, 2026, deadlines Mano tracks itself.** A deadline from a letter, a case or the person is stored on the person's agent in Engine, one per idempotency key, and Mano texts it on its own 14, 7 and 2 days before and on the day, at 10:00 in the zone of the person's state (Pacific when unknown). A deadline learned late gets one nudge at the next 10:00 unless a planned one is under 3 days away. Nudges are reminder rows, so they share the reminder delivery path (STOP, the conversation generation, the per-key dedupe), and they only go between 9:00 and 20:00 local on their own day; a nudge that misses its day is dropped rather than sent with a wrong count. Before every handoff, retries included, a nudge whose deadline closed, moved or was deleted is withdrawn. A nudge never carries a letter's own words: it names the action only from an explicit kind (renewal, recertification, interview, hearing, appeal, payment, documents) and the program only when it is a known one ("Your CalFresh renewal is due"); with no kind it names no action ("Your CalFresh deadline is in 12 days", or "the deadline from the letter you sent on" the day it arrived). The title is kept only for the agent, quoted as data. Every caller must send the conversation generation it prepared a deadline under. A nudge only offers the next step; acting still needs an approval. Deleting the conversation deletes the deadlines too, since they hold sentences from the person's letters.
- **October 30, 2026, the desktop never files.** The hosted desktop (`packages/computer`) fills a form and stops; filing happens only through Engine's action gateway after the person's yes, with the portal token the desktop never holds. The browser-filing executor is deleted. The computer-use loop never acknowledges a safety check: a pending check ends the run and Engine decides. There is no live view (VNC is removed), so a login prompt ends the run. Each desktop has a per-session secret on every control route, mounts only its own person's profile volume (named by an HMAC of the Engine person id), and reaches only the configured portal host, enforced by Modal's outbound domain allowlist and a Chromium URL policy.

- **November 2, 2026, the person's document list.** Engine records each document it stores for a person: when it arrived, the media type read from its bytes, its size, what Mail found it to be (a letter, whether or not Mail saved a deadline from it) and the conversation generation. The agent lists the current conversation's documents, newest first, each named in plain words by the day the person sent it in their own time zone ("the letter you sent on November 2"). Each listed document says whether the executor Engine is configured with could file it, by the same media type and size check the sandbox executor applies before filing (SANDBOX_MAX_UPLOAD_BYTES, 5 MiB by default; Engine itself stores up to 10 MB); simulated mode judges by the same limits, so it picks what a sandbox deploy would. A document stored before sizes were recorded counts as not fileable rather than reading its bytes back from R2 to measure it. A renewal attaches the document a profile value was read from if it is fileable, else the newest fileable one, and Mano asks for one only when none on file is, saying plainly whether the newest is too large or the wrong kind of file. The approval question names the attached document by that label, frozen into the draft, so the person knows what their yes sends. Deleting the conversation removes its documents from the list.
- **November 3, 2026, Guardian sees each action unredacted.** At David's request, after three review rounds on PR 108 kept finding the same thing: every value removed before Guardian is a place an attack can hide, and pattern matching cannot make removal both thorough and safe (a pattern that removes enough also erases an instruction written to look like a name, a street or a case number, and one that keeps every instruction misses values written a new way). Guardian receives each action as Mano would send it: the type, the recipient, the purpose, the payload with every field and its real value (a submit's name, birth date, address, phone, household size and income, and any text or claims), the document hash when a document is attached, every cited source with its quote, the consents on record, and whether the person or content asked. Nothing is removed or relabeled, and Engine no longer reads the profile at check time. Guardian is Meta's open-weight Muse Glimmer on our own Modal GPU. Our code stores nothing and logs no request or answer body (`packages/guardian/modal/app.py` has no prints of either, vLLM's offline engine there has no request logger, and errors name what failed, never the text). Modal kept the web function's inputs and outputs for up to 7 days until the entry below moved Guardian to a Modal Server, which Modal does not store. No rule forbids Guardian seeing the action, since it is the same content the person approves and the recipient receives. A personal form field that holds an email address or a link is still held before Guardian is asked, with a plain reason, because no real name, birth date, address or phone holds one; Guardian reading every field is the defense. Guardian's prompt treats the person's later yes as consent for the action's own recipient, needs no cited source for form answers, checks that the form fits its purpose and its numbers could be true, and blocks any other destination, an email address or a link that is not the recipient or consented, or an instruction in any field. This supersedes the redaction approach of PR 104 and of earlier rounds of PR 108.
- **November 3, 2026, Modal keeps no copy of what Guardian checks, once the old app is stopped.** At David's request (LAP-765), since Guardian now sees each action unredacted. Modal's security page (modal.com/docs/guide/security, Data retention) lists inputs and outputs of Functions, Web Functions included, as kept "Up to 7 days, then deleted", and request and response payloads of a Server as "Not stored" and "proxied directly to your container". It lists no workspace or app setting that shortens the 7 days. So Guardian is now one Modal Server (`@app.server`, named `check`) on the GPU in a new app, `mano-guardian-server`, running vLLM in-process behind the same single POST route, the same bearer token and Modal-Key/Modal-Secret proxy auth, and the same request and response bodies. There is no CPU web function in front and no `.remote` or `.spawn` call carrying an action, and the heartbeat Dict is gone. What the Server does not store: the action Engine sends, the verdict, and the model's review, by Modal or by our code (no prints, no log lines, uvicorn's access log off). What is stored: app and container logs of startup and errors, which name what failed and never the text (1 day on Starter, 30 on Team); Modal's app and container metadata; the model weights volume, shared by every Guardian app. The proxy terminates TLS and sees each request in flight. The old app, `mano-guardian`, is a web Function, and merging does not touch it, so Engine keeps working on its old URL. Until it is stopped, Modal keeps every input it receives for up to 7 days each, so the no-copy claim holds only after the steps below. Its old `mano-guardian-state` Dict held only a heartbeat time and is deleted by Modal 7 days after its last write. The order David follows: (1) merge, which deploys `mano-guardian-server` and prints its URL, `<workspace>--mano-guardian-server-check.us-east.modal.direct` (a Server answers on `modal.direct`; the same label on `modal.run` returns 404); (2) copy that URL into the `MANO_GUARDIAN_URL` GitHub secret and its Infisical copy, and into the agent's and the quality nightly's copies; (3) redeploy Engine; (4) check that Engine's `/health` shows `checker: guardian` and run one live check; (5) run the `guardian-modal` workflow from `main` with target `stop-old`, which stops `mano-guardian`. Inputs it already holds are deleted 7 days after each was received. Behavior changes with the Server: with no container up, Modal's proxy answers 503 at once instead of queueing, and Engine reads that as a cold start ("try again in a few minutes", and warm-up state `starting`). On staging the proxy also answered 503 once for about a second right after the container became ready, so a check that gets 503 retries once after a second when a full check (7 seconds) still fits its 10 second budget; a warm-up never retries. Staging measured cold starts of 186 and 209 seconds and warm checks of 4.4 to 6.3 seconds. The 10-minute warm hold is now the Server's `scaledown_window` of 600 seconds after the last request, and every check already sends a warm-up. The `guardian-modal` workflow's `staging` target deploys main's code to `mano-guardian-staging`, to check a merged change before Engine's URL moves to it. Every target is written to run only from `main`, since each holds production Modal credentials and branch code run with them could deploy over production. Each job's condition checks the ref and its first step checks it again in the shell, because GitHub's `==` ignores case and would admit a branch named `MAIN`. Jobs carry no services, container, env or other keys the runner would set up with a secret before that step. `guardian-check` compares the workflow's keys, permissions and triggers, and each job's keys, condition, runner, environment and first step, with an exact allowlist, which catches accidental edits on `main` and in reviewed pull requests. None of this stops a hostile branch, whose run uses its own copy of both workflows. Only the `guardian-production` environment's deployment branch policy, limited to `main`, closes that. That policy was set on November 3, 2026, with David's approval.
- **November 2, 2026, Mano asks instead of guessing, and the declaration is the person's.** Propel had general AI agents fill out the online SNAP application in two states (April 2026, an internal test with no real applicants and nothing submitted, https://www.propel.app/insights/can-ai-agents-help-people-apply-for-snap-part-1/): they guessed missing fields, cut a mistyped phone number to 10 digits instead of asking, and ticked the legal attestation themselves. Mano's renewal answers each one. Every value comes from the person's own answers or their profile (where a letter's facts land) and passes the same check either way; one that is missing or does not fit its field becomes one plain question, one at a time, in the form's order of importance: case number, name, date of birth, household size, income, address, phone, then the proof document. A value on file that does not fit is named in the question, never shortened or changed. Text goes to the portal exactly as given, less spaces around it; only dates and amounts are written the way the field stores them. A run of spaces inside a text value becomes one space when the value is taken, because a double space carries no meaning on a form and the text line shows it as one, so the person sees and approves the single-spaced value that is filed. A new call to fill the renewal from a later turn closes the version already shown before it asks anything, so a YES to that version never files a value the person has since corrected. A call that ends without holding a new version (it asks a question instead) also closes the drafts its own turn made, so the reply never carries a question for a version the person corrected in the same message. While a renewal the person already approved has no outcome yet (no receipt, and not failed), the form tools prepare nothing new, and Mano tells her plainly that it is still being checked with the office, that it will not be sent twice, and that she can change it once it settles; a later YES may still send the version she approved, once, under its key. Engine marks a filing failed only when the portal refused it or, through a compare-and-set on the attempt's version read before recovery's lookup and checks, when it was confirmed absent, so a receipt always wins and a request that moved the attempt keeps its outcome; a failed filing counts as settled and the pointer to its question is cleared, so a correction then goes through without another YES. Form calls within one turn run one at a time, and however a correcting call ends, a thrown error included, the drafts it was correcting are closed; one Engine does not confirm closed, after one retry, is held back, and one check (presentable) decides every question shown or counted as shown, so a held-back draft is never shown or made answerable. When a correction in a later turn cannot close the version she was shown, Mano tells her plainly that it still waits, that a YES would send it as it was, and to send the correction again; her next form call tries to close it again. A proposal a newer one replaced is never executed again: a replayed YES, a YES that waited on the safety check, and recovery all refuse it after reconciling, so a filing that went through still answers its receipt. A YES aimed at a question that has since been replaced shows nothing new: it says the question changed and nothing was sent, since only a question that went out with the reply of the turn that prepared it, and that Channels reported, can be answered. The in-memory hold-back stays as an extra guard, but safety does not depend on it. A form call that finds an approved filing whose post failed retires it in Engine before anything else, so a replayed YES cannot file it even when the correction only asks a question; Engine retires only an attempt still failed at the version it read, with no receipt (one a concurrent YES left in flight or unknown stays approved and the correction waits), a retired approval is still reconciled but never run again, and if the retire cannot be confirmed the tool prepares nothing and says so. A turn's reply shows a question only for a draft of that turn that its last form call for the form left waiting, read from the run's own tool results, so a replay that calls no tool shows nothing, a correction that failed shows nothing in any instance, and a result carried over from another turn is ignored. A receipt under the key wins over every refusal: every gateway answer about the person's own approval (superseded, retired, declined, already used) and every agent status read (the model's request status, the answers to YES and NO) reports it as sent, with its confirmation, whenever a receipt exists. A share's consent effect (a grant or revocation) is applied by the gateway in the same transaction that records the share's receipt and marks its attempt done, so no crash can leave a receipt whose effect never ran, and nothing applies it later on an answer. A grant's effect never applies once a revocation for that case is recorded, so a grant that reconciles after its revocation restores nothing. It is marked by action key in that transaction, so a later receipt for the same key (a late reconcile) applies nothing and a revoked consent stays revoked. Every start marks the share receipts already on file, since the earlier code applied those effects when they were recorded. Every form call first records, durably in Engine, that its turn is correcting the form: Engine then refuses drafts for that form from turns received before it, like a replaced draft, and closes those still waiting, so an older turn that resumes after a correction can never become the version a YES files. If the fence cannot be recorded, that exit cleans up like every other: the turn's earlier drafts for the form are closed or held back. A consent write that fails inside the settle transaction rolls the receipt, the done attempt and the marker back with it, so the next lookup applies it once; and a replayed filing answers its receipt before any fence refusal. Once every earlier filing for the form is settled (filed, retired or failed), a pointer to its question is cleared, so a correction goes ahead as a new filing. The renewal ends with the office's declaration under penalty of perjury. The draft freezes it word for word, the approval question shows it and says only the person can make it, and their YES makes it. Engine attaches who answered from the approval itself, never from caller input, and the sandbox executor files only with that answer from the applicant, sending the declaration as `{ statement, madeBy: "applicant" }`; the portal refuses any filing without it, and its browser pages have nothing to tick. Because a policy that allows submits without a YES has no answer to carry, such a policy can no longer file a renewal to the sandbox portal. The renewal also asks for the case number, read from the person's one food benefits case on file.
- **November 3, 2026, a texted photo is stored in a form the portal takes (LAP-743).** An iPhone camera saves photos as HEIC by default and iMessage can carry that file as is, and the sandbox portal takes only PDF, PNG and JPEG, so a HEIC letter could be read but never filed. Channels now reads each texted file's media type from its bytes, never from the provider's label, and stores a HEIC, HEIF or WebP photo as a JPEG copy (longest side at most 4096 pixels, quality 85) made by Cloudflare Images through the Worker's `IMAGES` binding. Images is a plain image service, not a model. A pure WASM decoder was measured and rejected: libheif's bundle is 2.0 MB (718 KB gzipped) before any JPEG encoder, which fits the Workers size limit, but decoding one synthetic 4032 by 3024 HEIC in Node on a laptop took about 220 ms of CPU and grew the process from 32 MB to about 182 MB (the decoder's heap plus a 48 MB RGBA copy), past the 128 MB a Worker isolate may use, and the decoder is LGPL. The real binding converted the same file in about 0.6 seconds of wall time, outside the Worker's CPU and memory, and gave identical bytes on a second run. Which form each file is stored in (the copy or the original) is saved in the conversation before the upload, and a replayed turn reuses it: a photo stored once is never converted again, one file never becomes two documents, and a copy that cannot be made again on replay stores nothing rather than the original. The ask for a screenshot is its own text after the reply, so the reply's length cap never drops it, and it is never added to a crisis reply. Logs name only a fixed set of bare media types, since a provider's label can carry the file's name. When no copy can be made (no binding, an Images error, a copy over 10 MB), Channels stores the photo as sent so Mail can still read it, and the same reply asks for a screenshot instead of leaving the problem for filing time. The conversion counts against Images' unique transformations.

- **November 3, 2026, a reminder the day before an interview or hearing (LAP-812).** The spine promises a reminder the day before Maria's interview, and the nudges were 14, 7 and 2 days before and on the day. A deadline the person shows up for (kind interview or hearing) is now nudged 14 and 7 days before, the day before and on the day, at the nudge time in the person's zone, through the same reminder path. The day before replaces 2 days out rather than joining it, so the person is not texted three days running. Paperwork kinds (renewal, recertification, redetermination, appeal, payment, documents) and deadlines with no kind keep 14, 7, 2 and the day of: the 2-day nudge leaves time to gather and send papers, which a day-before text would not add to. A nudge for a date the person shows up for only says when ("Your Medi-Cal interview is tomorrow."), since "is due" and "Want me to start it?" fit paperwork, not an appointment. Each send of the same deadline, a replay included, plans from the nudges stored for it, never from the kind it had before: the unsent ones stay, a day the kind no longer has is dropped, and a missing day is added while its nudge time is still ahead. Each nudge row records the due date it counts down to. A day handed off for a date, or cancelled by the person, is never planned again for that date at any revision, and a waiting nudge for a settled day is dropped rather than kept, so flipping the kind or moving the date away and back cannot send the same nudge twice. Nudges a change replaces are marked withdrawn, not cancelled, so a cancelled nudge of an open deadline is always one the person stopped. An interview or hearing tracked before this change moves to the new days and words the next time its key is sent. Rows stored before the due date column keep it empty rather than guessing it from their window, since the person's zone may have changed since; such a row that was handed off or cancelled settles its day count for that deadline whatever the date. That is conservative: it may skip a nudge, never repeat one.
- **November 3, 2026, a due date's year is checked against today, not read from the person's words (LAP-874).** A production trace saved "November 24" as 2024-11-24, which plans no nudges; reading years out of the person's words kept finding new gaps in review, so the tools use only the ISO date the model passes (anything else asks), today in the zone of the person's state, and `yearConfirmed`.
A date ahead within 183 days is saved. A past date moves to its next occurrence if that is within 183 days, with a note to state the year, else asks. A date further ahead asks with both dates when a nearer occurrence is still to come, else is saved with the note. `yearConfirmed`, set only after the person answers, saves the date as given. A date past Engine's two-year limit asks, confirmed or not. A wrong reminder the person can correct is cheap; a lost deadline is not.
Every saved date is said with its year, and the model is told today's date each turn. Mail reads a letter's date from its own text, so an old letter's past date stays on record as "was due" and the reply says no reminder will go out.
- **November 3, 2026, Mano never sets an iMessage chat background (LAP-850).** David's decision: the background Mano could put on a person's thread looked bad and is not set again. The upload path, its settings (`MANO_CHAT_BACKGROUND_ENABLED`, `MANO_CHAT_BACKGROUND_URL`) and the Channel method that set one are deleted, so no setting can turn it back on; it had been off by default since October 29, but threads, the maintainer's included, got it before then. Blooio's v4 API can remove one (`DELETE /chats/{chatId}/background`), so a chat whose stored record says an earlier version confirmed it set a background has it removed once, after the next reply reaches the person, outside the turn, never on a crisis turn, after STOP or on a closed chat. Removing is idempotent, so a failed try is repeated on a later reply, at most 3 times per chat, the cap the uploads had. The `chat_backgrounds` table stays in each conversation's SQLite and records the removal (`clearing`, then `cleared`); nothing deletes its rows, and it is kept across a delete so the removal runs at most once. A record left uncertain (an upload whose outcome was unknown) is left alone: that upload may have failed and the person may have set their own background since, and removing theirs is worse than leaving one of ours in place.
